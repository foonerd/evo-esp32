# Waveshare ESP32-C5-Touch-LCD-2.8

The player's firmware for its first board, and the fabric's first run on a chip. On boot it brings the IO expander up with the amplifier enabled, opens the ES8311, starts I2S0 as master on the vendor BSP's pins, mirrors the three signals onto header pins a Pi-HAT DAC can reach, brings up an ES9018K2M if one answers on the HAT's I2C, Then it mounts the card, stands the fabric up as the host distribution does (the catalogue compiled in and checked against the plugins, the files source over the card, the Symphonia decoder, the null output) and plays every track on the card once, shuffled, through the I2S sink, with the face on the panel (`distributions/face`, the consumer the host draws as a PNG) refreshed every 40 ms (a full frame in ten stages with a decoder tick between each, so a track change does not starve the ring) and answering touch (play pauses and resumes, previous and next skip, the volume strip follows the finger, a lift on the ring seeks), the album's `cover.jpg` (or `folder.jpg`, `albumart.jpg`, `front.jpg`) decoded on a worker thread beneath the main task and shown in the disc, and behind the navigation the Library (the card as the source lists it; a tap on a track makes its folder the queue from there) and Settings (the player's facts); every happening goes to the log, and a heartbeat every ten seconds says what the heap and the main stack have left. A card with no music runs the face all the same, saying so.

The sink (`src/sink.rs`) is I2S0 with its DMA as the buffer: the decoder offers frames, the sink hands the DMA what fits with a zero timeout and says how many frames that was, so the I2S clock paces playback and a tick never waits. The ring is sized in time, a quarter second at the stream's rate within 64 KB of internal SRAM (11 buffers at 44.1 kHz, 16 at 96 kHz); a stream at another rate has the channel rebuilt and the codec told. The sink also applies the level, a Q15 gain on the house curve (1..=100 as -49.5..0 dB in half-decibel steps), so the speaker and a HAT hear the same level; the DACs sit at their full scale. The heartbeat says how busy the core is, what SRAM is left, where the playback position stands against the clock (1.000 is keeping up) and whether the DMA ran dry. Measured: 96 kHz/24-bit FLAC decodes at 37-58% of the core and plays at 1.000; the face costs 1-6% (a full frame 421 ms end to end, no stage above 73 ms); MP3 through Espressif's fixed-point decoder (`platform/esp-idf`, preferred in the codec registry because the C5 has no FPU and Symphonia's floating-point MP3 reached 0.555 of real time) plays at 1.000 with the core 23-25% busy; 192 kHz FLAC decodes at 0.38 of real time and is beyond this chip.

| Part | Where |
| --- | --- |
| The board | the companion's `boards/waveshare-esp32-c5-touch-lcd-28` crate: I2C (SDA GPIO0, SCL GPIO1, 400 kHz) with the CH32V003 expander at `0x24` (EXIO3 enables the NS4150B amplifier), the ST7789 panel over SPI2 at 40 MHz, the CST3530 touch, the backlight |
| ES8311 codec | I2C `0x18`; no MCLK, clocked from the I2S bit clock; the companion's `es8311` driver |
| I2S0 | master, Philips, 16-bit stereo slots; BCLK GPIO24, WS GPIO25, data to the codec GPIO26 |
| I2S0, mirrored for a Pi-HAT DAC | the same three signals through the GPIO matrix: BCLK GPIO2 (J8 pin 7), WS GPIO3 (J8 pin 8), data GPIO11 (J6 TXD); the console is on USB so J6 is free. The speaker and the HAT play the same frames. |

Wiring a HAT, with pin 1 as the silkscreen marks it: J8.7 → HAT pin 12 (BCLK), J8.8 → HAT pin 35 (LRCLK), J6 TXD → HAT pin 40 (DIN), J8.1 or J6 GND → HAT pin 6, J8.2 (USB 5 V) → HAT pin 2. A bare PCM5102A module takes the same three signals plus VIN 5 V and GND, with its SCK pin tied to GND.

The build is the companion's: ESP-IDF v5.5.5 through esp-idf-svc 0.53, nightly-2026-10-17 with `build-std`, `ldproxy`, `partitions.csv` with the board's 32 MB of flash declared and one 8 MB app partition, `scripts/image.sh` for the flashable image. The companion's drivers come by its tag.

```sh
cd firmware/waveshare-esp32-c5-touch-lcd-28
cargo build --release
cd ../.. && scripts/image.sh firmware/waveshare-esp32-c5-touch-lcd-28
esptool --chip esp32c5 --port PORT write-flash 0x0 dist/evo-esp32-waveshare-esp32-c5-touch-lcd-28-0.1.0.bin
```

## Configuring by card

The player reads `evoconfig.txt` from the root of the card on every boot and writes `evostatus.txt` next to it with what it applied and what it did not understand. Copy [`sdcard/evoconfig.txt`](../../sdcard/evoconfig.txt) to the card and edit it in any text editor; without the file the defaults stand (Evo Default, the disc, rotation 0, 40%). One `key = value` per line, UTF-8, CRLF or LF, `#` or `;` comments on their own lines only, a value in double quotes keeps its spaces, an empty value is the default again. The keys: `theme` (evo-default, night-sky, sunrise, air, liquid), `style` (disc, card), `rotation` (0, 90, 180, 270; applied at boot), `volume` (0 to 100 at power-on), `name` (the hostname the router is told; `evo-player` when not set), `wifi_ssid` and `wifi_password` (the network to join; see below), `dac` and `output` (read and reported; acted on once the register of DAC boards lands). A tap on `Theme ›` or `Style ›` in Settings changes the face and writes the key back into the file in place, leaving every other line as written; a card without the file gets one. `Rotation ›` writes the next quarter turn and restarts the player, which comes up turned. The password is on the card in clear, as it is for the companion; the card stays in the player.

With `wifi_ssid` set, the player joins the network on a thread of its own, so the music does not wait for it: a network seen before is joined in about three seconds (the companion's `WifiNet` remembers the access point's channel and BSSID in NVS), a new one by a search of both bands in about fourteen. The address and band are a row of Settings (`Wi-Fi`) and a `wifi:` line of `evostatus.txt`, rewritten when the link comes up or drops; a dropped link is rejoined. 2.4 and 5 GHz, WPA2 and WPA3. Internal SRAM is the price: the driver and its pools take about 110 KB of it (half as code the C5 keeps resident), so the firmware places its own heap by size (`alloc.rs`: records and text in PSRAM, the decoder's working buffers in SRAM), keeps the art fetcher's stack in PSRAM, and runs the Wi-Fi driver's code from flash; `memory:` lines at boot and the heartbeat's `internal` figure say what is left (about 58 KB with a 96 kHz stream and the network up). The join's key exchange, like anything else that holds the core for a while, is covered by the sink's reservoir: the decoder runs up to 600 ms ahead into PSRAM and a feeder task above the Wi-Fi driver carries the audio to the DMA (`sink.rs`).

Internet radio comes from playlists on the card. A `.m3u`, `.m3u8` or `.pls` file anywhere on the card lists stations (`#EXTINF:-1,Name` above each URL, or `FileN=`/`TitleN=` pairs); they appear in the library in that folder under the playlist's name, and a tap plays one once the network is up. [`sdcard/Radio/Evo Selection.m3u`](../../sdcard/Radio/Evo%20Selection.m3u) is a checked selection of UK stations to begin with. A `tvg-logo="…"` attribute on the `#EXTINF` line (the IPTV convention) names the station's picture, a URL or a path on the card relative to the playlist, PNG or JPEG; it is set whole on a square of the theme's background (a cover is cropped to its centre), and a station without one shows its name in the art square. The stream is fetched by a thread of its own into a 512 KB ring in PSRAM, a second and a half ahead before the first note, so the decoder never waits on the network; the station's name is the album line, and where the server sends what is playing (Icecast and SHOUTcast metadata) the title and artist follow the songs; a line too long for its width scrolls as a loop. `http://` and `https://` (the certificate bundle is built in, and redirects are followed); MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis. **AAC is not decoded on this board**: Symphonia's AAC decoder is built on the stack, tens of kilobytes of it, and overflows the main task's 32 KB, so the firmware builds the playback plugin without it; a station whose address ends in `.aac` is greyed in the library and one that turns out to be AAC when opened is refused with "format not supported here". A station that will not open stays on the face with the reason on the status strip rather than the player moving on to another station unasked; a file that will not open is passed over as before. Stations have no end, so the boot's run through the card leaves them out; a stream cannot be seeked. Threads the plugins spawn (the stream's fetcher) get their stacks in PSRAM; expect the heartbeat's `internal` figure to drop by about 20 KB while a station plays.

Known of the chip: no FPU. Software `sin` costs about 95 µs a call, so the tone is one exact cycle (441 samples) tiled, not 88 200 sines; the first build did the latter and the idle task's watchdog said so.

Known of ESP-IDF: below one scheduler tick, `usleep()` spins instead of yielding, so at the default 100 Hz every sleep under 10 ms starves the idle task and its watchdog bites five seconds later. `sdkconfig.defaults` sets `CONFIG_FREERTOS_HZ=1000`; the player's loop is paced in milliseconds. The main task's stack is 48 KB; the heartbeat reports its low-water mark (45 KB free with the decoder running: Symphonia's state lives on the heap).
